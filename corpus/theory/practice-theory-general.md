# Practice Theory (General)

## Core concepts

Practices as embodied, materially mediated arrays of activity; competences, meanings,
and materials; social reproduction through routine enactment.

## Analytical job

Explain how stable patterns of action persist and change without reducing them to
individual attitudes or macro structures alone.

## Operationalization signals

- Analysis moves from individuals to **practices** (eating, driving, parenting, etc.)
- Attention to materials, skills, and meanings together
- Findings link micro conduct to broader social arrangements

## Weak use patterns

- "Practice" as synonym for "behavior" or "experience"
- Listing practices without explaining their composition or linkage
- Theoretical introduction with no practice-level analysis in findings

## Overclaim patterns

- Claiming practice change from thin or cross-sectional evidence
- Universalizing one site as general social transformation

## Pairings and tensions

Often paired with STS, consumption studies, and sustainability transitions.
Distinct from pure Bourdieusian field analysis when practice arrays are the unit.

## Reviewer questions checklist

- Is "practice" analytically defined or used loosely?
- Do findings show how practices are constituted and linked?

## Qualisapio notes

Common in UK/European qualitative sociology — check operationalization, not vocabulary.
